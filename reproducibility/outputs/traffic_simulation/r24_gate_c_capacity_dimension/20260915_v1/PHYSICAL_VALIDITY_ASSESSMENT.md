# Physical validity assessment

## Mass

Mass represents the legal/manufacturer payload constraint directly. The selected kei-class references publish variant-specific payload values, but R24 demand has no matching kg. Japanese air-cargo ratios, service maximums and foreign distributions cannot be applied as an Ota residential B2C mean without a transfer model. Mass might be non-binding for small instances; that does not make it invalid and is not a reason to inflate demand.

## Volume

Volume is plausibly important for low-density parcels, but both sides are incomplete. Published internal length/width/height are geometric limits, not verified usable cubic capacity after wheel housings, trim, access, stacking and loading practices. The demand has no parcel dimensions or volume. Simple bounding-box products are rejected.

## Parcel count

The saved demand connects exactly to `parcel_equivalent`, but this is a synthetic abstract content count rather than identified physical parcels. A van's physical parcel count depends on size mix, shape, stacking and loading policy. No manufacturer parcel-count capacity exists in the reviewed evidence. It is useful only for a separately labeled methodological test with explicit arbitrary capacity.

## Standardized load unit

Japanese government standardization material concerns pallets and unitized upstream/warehouse freight. It does not define a residential B2C parcel load unit or a kei-van `Q`. A new LU normalization would be reproducible but not externally grounded; it is rejected for the R24 physical primary.

## Multi-dimensional capacity

Mass plus volume is physically strongest in principle, compatible with VRPTW/EVRP extensions, but current missing data would multiply assumptions. It is deferred until both dimensions have coherent evidence. R24 standard CVRP continuity favors a single scalar dimension once authority exists.
